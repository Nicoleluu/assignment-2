WHAT COUNTS
AS GOOD
DESIGN?

We often call something “well-designed” without asking why. Is it because it works? Because it looks beautiful? Because it feels right?

Beauty / Use

If something is beautiful but difficult to use, does it still count as good design?

Some believe beauty creates meaning. Others believe usefulness is everything.

Intuition / Testing

Should designers trust their instincts?

Intuition shapes ideas. Testing challenges them. A thoughtful process often means accepting that your first assumption may be wrong.

Friction / Ease

Should good design always feel effortless?

Some experiences become meaningful because they require patience, slowness, and effort.

Research / Bias

Can research ever be neutral?

Every method, every question, and every sample carries assumptions. Good designers don’t eliminate bias. They learn to recognize it.

Power / Access

Who gets considered in the design process?

Good design for one person may exclude another. Design is never neutral.

Build Your Design Constellation

So what matters most to you?

There is no universal answer. Only the values you choose to design with.

Maybe good design isn’t about finding the right answer— but understanding what matters enough to guide your choices.
